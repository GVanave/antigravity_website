Bachelors & Masters

Navigate the German university system with ease. Public universities offer world-class degrees in Engineering, IT, and Business—typically with zero tuition fees. Ausbildung (Vocational Training)

Earn while you learn. This 3-4 year program combines practical work at a German company (3-4 days/week) with theoretical study at a vocational school (1-2 days/week). You receive a monthly stipend (approx. €850 to €1,200+) with zero tuition fees. High demand exists in Healthcare, IT, Mechatronics, and Logistics. A minimum B1 German proficiency is generally required.

Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card)

Germany's new points-based visa system for skilled professionals. Enter Germany for up to one year without a pre-existing job offer to search for employment. You can work part-time (up to 20 hours/week) while looking. Eligibility requires a recognized degree or at least two years of vocational training, plus basic German (A1) or English (B2) skills.

Why Germany

New 2024 Immigration Laws

Recent changes have drastically reduced the time to settle. Graduates can now apply for permanent residency in just 21 months (with B1 German), and the pathway to naturalization has been cut to just 5 years.

Accessible EU Blue Card

Salary thresholds for the EU Blue Card have been significantly lowered for recent graduates and STEM professionals. This makes it much easier to transition straight from a student visa into long-term skilled employment.

Expanded Work Rights

International students are now legally permitted to work 140 full days (or 280 half-days) per year. With a high statutory minimum wage, students can comfortably cover their own living expenses while studying.
